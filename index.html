The framework

10 signals. Every one
anchored in science.

Not aesthetic opinions. Documented mechanisms of how the human brain processes advertising. Each signal scores something specific and tells you exactly what it is costing you.

  • 01
    Selective Attention
    The Interrupt
    The brain filters 11 million bits of input per second and consciously registers 40. Does this creative force the brain to stop — or pass through completely unregistered?
  • 02
    Affective Priming
    The Opening Emotion
    Emotion fires 150 milliseconds before language. What does the viewer feel before they read a single word — and is that emotion moving them toward the product or away?
  • 03
    Self-Concept Theory
    The Mirror
    People do not buy products. They buy versions of themselves. Does this creative speak to a specific identity — or just a demographic?
  • 04
    Cognitive Recognition
    The Truth
    A sharp insight triggers dopamine release and immediate trust. Is the truth in this ad proprietary — or can any competitor run it by swapping their logo?
  • 05
    Zeigarnik Effect
    The Open Loop
    The brain has a deep compulsion toward closure. Does this creative open a genuine cognitive loop the viewer must close — or is it declarative and instantly forgettable?
  • 06
    Attentional Flow
    The Eye Path
    The eye follows a hierarchy determined by size, contrast and position. Does that sequence match the persuasion order — or is the creative working against itself?
  • 07
    Approach Motivation
    The Pull
    Desire is not need. Does this creative make the viewer want the product — or just understand it? Does reward anticipation activate before they finish reading?
  • 08
    Processing Fluency
    The Weight
    Working memory holds 3 to 4 pieces of information at scroll speed. Is the copy asking for more than the brain can hold — causing avoidance before conversion?
  • 09
    Credibility Assessment
    The Proof
    Every claim triggers an automatic credibility check. Are claims specific enough to be believed — or generic enough to be dismissed before the viewer is conscious of it?
  • 10
    Brand Imprint
    The Ownership
    Remove the logo. Does the brand still exist in the frame? A creative with no imprint could belong to any competitor. One with strong imprint is completely ownable.
